# Physical interaction overhaul

Pavlov Shack style body holsters and reloading, Half-Life 2 VR style melee, grenades and scopes, true akimbo, consumable gestures, networked full-body IK and a wrist HUD. Every mechanic works on three kinds of server:

| Server | What you get |
|---|---|
| Listen server hosted by a VR player running this build | Full fidelity: the host's hooks apply melee sweeps along the swung blade, controller-ray bullets, hand-velocity throws, physical magazine and shell transactions. |
| Dedicated server running the `l4d2vr_server` plugin | The same, server-side. The plugin advertises what it supports with `l4d2vr_feature_ack`. |
| Any other server | Client-side approximations that only use standard input (weapon slots, view angles, buttons). Desktop players and servers see a normal client. |

Desktop players running the mod are unaffected; they can join VR servers and play alongside VR players. Without a headset the mod starts in desktop mode by itself: with no usable OpenXR runtime it never tries VR, and if VR fails to start it continues on the desktop instead of showing an error, saying why in `vrmod_log.txt` and the game console (`DesktopModeAuto=true`; `-nohmd` still runs the game fully vanilla). Desktop players with the mod see VR players' bodies.

The mod uses OpenXR only (see [openxr.md](openxr.md): requirements per platform, what changed for players, the messages when VR does not start, and the OpenXR headset checklist). It talks to the runtime through one backend seam (`L4D2VR/vr_backend/`). It needs `openxr_loader.dll` next to `d3d9.dll` (shipped with the mod) and an active 32-bit OpenXR runtime with Vulkan: SteamVR 2.17 or later, Virtual Desktop or Meta Quest Link on Windows; on Linux, Proton Hotfix or Experimental with `PROTON_USE_WOW64=1`, a Steam launch and an OpenVR runtime such as SteamVR or XRizer still registered (Proton needs it before its OpenXR bridge starts). `VrRuntime` in `vr/config.txt` and the `-l4d2vr_openxr` / `-l4d2vr_openvr` launch options only matter in legacy developer builds that still contain the OpenVR backend (`/p:L4D2VRLegacyOpenVR=true`); the release logs `[VR] VR runtime: OpenXR (VrRuntime=auto); OpenXR-only build`. The OpenXR backend logs `[VR][OpenXR]` lines (runtime registration, session state, frame counters every 5 s).

The separate SteamVR glove models (`VrHandsGlovesEnabled`) are retired: the setting is ignored and every player, left-handed included, uses the survivor's own hands.

## Holsters

Reach to a holster with either hand and squeeze its grip: the item is in that hand immediately, with a haptic pulse, and the hovered item glows.

Grips are toggles, as in Pavlov (`GripToggle`, on by default). One firm squeeze takes an item and your hand keeps it after you let go; on Index controllers that means a hard squeeze, not a light grip. The next squeeze lets go of it:

- inside the item's own holster, it is put away;
- over another holster, you swap to that item;
- anywhere else, it is dropped.

A squeeze that takes nothing is ignored: only an item the grip itself took (drawn from a holster or picked up with the grip) can be dropped by letting go. A gun you spawned with, picked up with Use or switched to stays in your hand however often you squeeze. With `GripToggle=false` you hold the grip to keep an item instead. The two-handed foregrip is also a toggle: squeeze once to take the front of the gun and again to let go (`VrHandsTwoHandedGripHeldMode=false`). Magazines, bolts and slides are worked with the off-hand trigger and held only while you keep it pulled (see Reloading).

The layout copies Pavlov VR's body inventory, read from the game's own data (`research/pavlov/inventory/SPEC.md`):

- **Root:** an inventory root hangs 75 cm below the headset. It moves back a little when you look down, and up to 60 cm back when your off hand reaches to the floor.
- **Facing:** the root turns with your head continuously (`HolsterYawFollowSpeed`, 20 as in Pavlov), and stick or snap turns move it instantly.
- **Hand reach:** your hand counts as an 11.5 cm sphere.

| Holster | Where | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Back, over the dominant shoulder | Dominant half of a 40 cm sphere behind your head (follows your head) | Primary weapon, slung upright with the muzzle up by that shoulder |
| Back, over the off shoulder | Off half of the same sphere | Medkit, defibrillator or upgrade pack, strapped upright on the shoulder blade |
| Dominant hip | Upright capsule beside the root (radius 20 cm, 88 cm tall) | Pistol(s) or melee weapon; the pistol sits muzzle down, grip up and back |
| Chest, off-hand side (upper pocket) | Reach for the item | Grenade, upright |
| Chest, dominant side (upper pocket) | Reach for the item | Pills or adrenaline, upright |
| Off-hand hip | Reach for it | Spare magazine (or a shell for shotguns) while you have reserve ammo |

Either hand can reach over either shoulder: the side of your head the hand is on picks the item, so reaching over your dominant shoulder never draws the medkit and an empty half draws nothing. Left-handed mode mirrors everything. Order when zones overlap: a chest item within reach, then the dominant hip, then the off-hand hip (the magazine pouch, which never draws a holster item), then the back. Chest items go back in when your hand is anywhere in the vest, a capsule over the chest; a back item goes back in its own half, or anywhere behind you while the other half is empty. Without the first-person body the back items aren't drawn, because nothing would hide them, but they can still be grabbed. Other settings: `HolsterPavlovLayoutEnabled`, `HolsterItemsVisible`, `HolsterInstantDraw`, `HolsterHeightScale`, `HolsterHoverHapticAmplitude` and `HolsterGrabHapticAmplitude`.

Each grip press logs where your hand was relative to the head (`[VR][Holster] grip pressed`), so the layout can be checked against your own reaches.

No weapon switch plays the draw animation (`HolsterInstantDraw`): holster draws, pickups, slot keys, the switch after a throw or a medkit and switches the server makes all put the item straight into your hand. The weapon is shown in its idle pose while the game's draw plays underneath; the very first time a model is drawn, before its idle pose is known, it is not drawn until the draw has settled (up to about a second), and the learned poses are kept in `VR\viewmodel_idle_poses.bin` for later sessions. On servers with the plugin the deploy delay before you can fire is cleared too, also for draws made between commands (the `use` command a holster draw sends); elsewhere the server's own delay still applies.

## Ambidextrous items

The hand that takes an item holds and uses it (`AmbidextrousItemHands`, on by default). Draw a gun with your left hand and it sits in your left hand, aims along your left controller and fires with your left trigger; a grenade drawn with the left hand is armed with the left trigger and thrown with the left hand; pills go to your mouth and adrenaline into your other arm from the left. Your other hand becomes the support hand: it takes the foregrip, magazines, slides and bolts, the magazine pouch moves to its hip, and its trigger no longer fires. The ammo display follows the hand with the gun. Holsters stay where they are. The item is held by the survivor's own right-hand model on your left controller.

- **Drawing:** squeeze over a holster with the free hand: the item goes into that hand, exactly as a draw with the gun hand (glow, haptics, put away or drop with the next squeeze).
- **Handing over:** bring the free hand to the item in the other hand and squeeze (hands within `ItemHandOffRadiusMeters`, 14 cm); a squeeze on the foregrip, a magazine, the bolt or slide, or a holster does that instead. Or hold the foregrip with the free hand and let go with the item hand: the item stays in the hand on the foregrip instead of dropping.
- Both triggers and grips trade roles with the item; sticks and face buttons stay where your bindings put them (the magazine release stays on the right A button). Desktop players and servers see a normal player whose shots come from that hand.

Dropping (`HolsterReleaseDrop`, on by default): letting go of an item you took with the grip drops it with your hand's motion, so you can toss a gun to a teammate. Letting go inside or over a holster never drops it. Items you already held before drawing aren't affected. Dropping needs a server with the plugin or a modded host; elsewhere letting go does nothing. A dropped gun lands at your feet, and L4D2 picks a weapon up again when you stand on it with that slot empty; if it comes back to your hand that way (or the host refuses the drop), your grip holds it again, so the next squeeze lets it go.

## Reloading

Grips grab, triggers interact, as in Pavlov VR (`MagazineInteractionTriggerInteractions`, on by default). With a gun in your hand, your free hand's trigger does the small jobs: it takes a magazine or shell from the pouch, seats it, pulls the seated magazine out, and grabs the slide, bolt, charging handle or pump. Keep the trigger pulled to hold what it took and let go to release it. The free hand's grip does the grabbing: holsters, the front of a two-handed gun, and items in the world. That trigger never fires the gun or shoves; shoves come from moving the gun or a melee weapon (`PhysicalShoveEnabled`). With `MagazineInteractionTriggerInteractions=false` the off-hand grip does both jobs and the left trigger shoves again, unless `MotionOnlyMeleeAndShove` is on. A left-held pistol and akimbo pistols keep the grip-based reload, because their left trigger fires.

- **Release:** the Magazine Release button (right A by default) drops detachable magazines. The AK-47's magazine is grabbed and pulled out by hand instead (`MagazineReleaseButtonRequired`). A magazine drops once: while it is out, pressing release again does nothing, even after switching weapons. Its rounds go back to your reserve and a loaded gun keeps the round in its chamber; the fresh magazine then fills from the reserve.
- **Fresh magazine:** put your off hand on the magazine pouch at your off-hand hip (with the Pavlov holster layout; otherwise at `MagazineInteractionFreshMagazinePickupOffsetMeters`), pull the trigger and insert it into the magazine well. The spare (or shell) sits in your hand the way it sits in the gun: hold your off hand like your gun hand (pointing along the barrel, thumb up) and bring it up a little nose-high under the well, and it lines up (Pavlov VR leans held magazines forward 15 degrees, 5 for pistols, the Uzi and the MAC-10). `ManualReloadMagazineHandRotationOffsetDeg` turns it further in the hand. Only the fresh magazine refills the gun, from your reserve; the stock reload never runs on its own, so an empty gun stays empty until a new magazine is in.
- **Magazine slap** (`MagazineSlapEnabled`, on by default; AK-47, SG552 and military sniper, like Pavlov's AK, Galil and AutoSniper): you can take the spare while the old magazine is still in; the old one glows. Strike it out with the fresh magazine: hit it so it is pushed out of the well (roughly downward on a level gun) or rocked toward the muzzle (the AK forward slap), as described below. The old magazine drops as with any release (one ejection, rounds back to the reserve), and the fresh one can go in 0.15 s later. The slap is physical. The fresh and the seated magazine are boxes from their rendered bones. Each frame the fresh one is swept along its path since the last frame, relative to the gun, in small steps, so a fast strike can't pass through the old magazine between frames, and walking or turning never counts. The first touch gives the face that was hit and the strike's speed into it. The old magazine can only leave its well out of the well, rocked toward the muzzle, or in between. So it gets the part of the strike's push into that face that points along one of those directions, plus friction (up to half of the push) dragging along the face. It is knocked out when that speed reaches 90 % of Pavlov's minimum (speed × direction): 0.43 m/s on the AK-47 and 0.55 m/s on the others. A glancing touch, a push toward the stock or into the well, a slow push, or a jump faster than 6 m/s (tracking, not a hand) never knocks it out. Below the threshold the fresh magazine rests on the old one's surface instead of passing through it. You feel a light tick on contact and a strong pulse on the slap. The old magazine flies out along the direction it was knocked, with the gun's motion plus 80 % of the strike (at least 0.6 m/s), then falls like any dropped magazine. Every touch logs `[VR][MagazineSlap] touch:` with each velocity estimate, and a touch without a slap logs `[VR][MagazineSlap] the spare hit the old magazine without a slap` with the speeds and the face. Letting go of the trigger puts the spare back (after 0.12 s, so a hand that relaxes mid-swing keeps it). On the SG552 and sniper, the release button also drops the old magazine while you hold the spare.
- **Pulling the magazine out by hand** (AK-47, SG552, military sniper): with your free hand empty, pull the trigger on the seated magazine and pull it out of the well. It comes out like any released magazine: one ejection, and its rounds go back to the reserve. It stays in your hand while you hold the trigger. To reseat it, pull it clear (6 cm out along the well) and push it back in (3 cm from the furthest point), so it never goes back in while you are still pulling it out. Or let go away from the gun to drop it. When your hand is near both the seated magazine and the pouch, the closer one wins. On the SG552 and sniper, this needs `MagazineInteractionTriggerInteractions`, because there the old grip-based pull would fight the foregrip.
- **Chamber:** if the gun was empty, pull the slide, bolt, charging handle or pump to chamber the first round. Racking only chambers a round from a seated magazine: with the empty magazine still in, or with no magazine, it does nothing. Pump shotguns need a pump after each shot (`ManualPumpEnabled`); shells can be loaded before or after the pump, one shell per insertion. A shot the game showed but the host never fired (the trigger while switching items or right after a draw) needs no pump or rack, even when the gun is drawn again later: the shells come back with the reserve unchanged. While the pump or a reload waits, the gun is held still in its resting pose in your hand (never a frame of the draw or fire animation).
- **Empty guns** never reload or eject on their own; the magazine glows instead (`MagazineEmptyGlowEnabled`).
- **Reload hints:** whatever the next step needs glows softly. That is the magazine when the gun is empty, the spare magazine once the old one is out, and the slide, bolt, charging handle or pump while the gun waits to be cycled. All of these use the `MagazineEmptyGlowEnabled` setting.
- **Released magazines** fall to the floor and disappear after a while (`MagazineDropEnabled`, `MagazineDropLifetimeSeconds`).
- **Pavlov tuning** (`ReloadPavlovTuning`, on by default): each gun uses Pavlov VR's numbers for how far you pull the slide, bolt, charging handle or pump to cock it, where the round chambers as it goes forward (or let go after a full pull and it snaps forward), and how deep a magazine must go in. Examples: the M16 cocks at 7 cm and chambers below 4.9 cm, pistols cock at 7.6 cm, and the AK at 9.5 cm; pulls are capped at 10 cm. The slide model moves only its own travel. The magazine well accepts a magazine within 10 cm and 45 degrees, facing the way it goes in (not backwards or upside down), and the spare magazine can be grabbed with the hand within 20 cm of the pouch (plus the hand's 11.5 cm radius with the Pavlov layout); a hand on the slide, bolt or pump racks it instead. Your per-weapon `MagazineInteraction...Overrides` still win.
- **Pouch glow:** while a fresh magazine is needed, the spare glows; within 35 cm of your empty off hand it glows brighter and you feel a tick.
- **Speed:** on servers with physical transactions (listen host or plugin) ammo is committed when the magazine seats, so you can reload faster than the animation. On other servers the stock reload runs when the fresh magazine seats, so its timer still applies (`PhysicalReloadOnUnsupportedServers`).

## Akimbo

With dual pistols, each trigger fires its own pistol along its own hand. On servers without the plugin each shot's view angles are aimed from your eye at the point that hand's ray hits (`DualPistolsIndependentHandsEnabled`, `DualPistolsAkimboOnAnyServer`).

## Melee and shoving

- Melee swinging is modelled on Ancient Dungeon VR: its sword for the long weapons, its dagger for the short ones (`MeleeInertiaEnabled`). The handle stays welded to your hand and only the weapon's rotation lags, pivoting at your grip. The lag grows with how fast you turn the weapon: about 11 degrees at 500 deg/s and 20 to 25 degrees at 900 deg/s, and it is softly limited there (15 degrees for the knife, 25 for the head-heavy weapons). There is no speed cap: a fast swing never falls further and further behind. Long weapons build the lag up more slowly and settle with a slight follow-through; short ones snap back without overshooting. Each weapon gets its own spring from its reach (see "Melee weapon table" below). The visible weapon, the server sweep and the swing detector all use the same lagged weapon.
- A swing has to be fast and committed. It counts (and can hit) while the blade, measured about three quarters of the way to its tip (at least 0.5 m out), moves at least 4.5 m/s relative to your head and your hand at least 2.5 m/s (3.0 m/s for the short weapons: knife, tonfa, machete, frying pan, riot shield), once the blade has travelled 0.43 m and the hand 0.13 m over the last 0.6 s. Speeds are averaged over about 45 ms. Wrist flicks, slow swings and short pokes never count. The hit window closes when the blade drops below 2.5 m/s or the hand below 1.25 m/s, and a backhand can follow a forehand straight away (at most one new swing every 0.35 s, the server's own melee gate). On servers with the L4D2VR plugin or a modded host, the server's sweep only runs inside that window, so hits land where and when the swing is valid; on plain servers the swing start presses the stock attack. The gates are in config.txt (`MeleeSwingMinTipSpeed`, `MeleeSwingMinHandSpeedLong`, `MeleeSwingMinHandSpeedShort`, `MeleeSwingMinTipTravel`, `MeleeSwingMinHandTravel`). Each swing logs `[VR][Melee] swing` with its speeds, travel, lag and Ancient Dungeon's hit quality; fast motions that did not count log `[VR][Melee] swing candidate` with the gate values (at most once a second). The chainsaw only needs the trigger.
- You feel the hit, not the swing: a confirmed melee hit pulses the controller at full strength for 70 ms (`melee.hit` in haptics_config.txt). `MeleeSwingStartHaptics=true` adds the old pulse when a swing starts (`melee.swing`). The game's own swing sound still plays when a swing starts.
- Thrusting a melee weapon forward, or pushing a gun forward along its barrel (a gun bash), shoves in that direction (`PhysicalShoveEnabled`). A fast, level, sideways swing of the gun in front of the body (about 2.4 m/s at the hand) also bashes and shoves toward the gun (`GunBashSweepEnabled`). Reaching for a holster, drawing, aiming, reloading, flicking the wrist or turning the whole body with the gun raised is never a bash.
- Strikes are measured from the controllers' real motion relative to the head in tracking space. Smooth and snap turning with the stick, stick walking, teleports, ladders and being pushed move the whole play space, never the hands relative to it, so they can never bash or swing a melee weapon (earlier builds measured in a frame pivoted at the camera anchor and timed samples with a float of the time since boot, so after a few days of uptime turning with the stick bashed). Each bash logs `[VR][Melee] gun bash push|sweep` with its speeds and the stick turn rate; fast motions that did not count log `[VR][Melee] bash candidate` (at most once a second) with the gate values. The off-hand outward swing shove (`MotionGestureSwingThreshold`) is also measured relative to the head, in the controller's own tracking-space direction, and is off while the off hand holds the gun or works a reload.
- The game's own shove and swing animations never play: your hand stays where your controller is, while the shove's sound and hits still happen.

### Melee weapon table

The lag spring of each weapon has a natural frequency of about 11.1 / reach Hz (an effective reach: blades x 1.0, blunt x 1.1, head-heavy x 1.3, kept between 8 and 17 Hz) and a damping ratio of 0.072 x the frequency, so damping over stiffness stays about 23 ms and every weapon lags the same amount at a given speed; heavier ones take longer to build it up and to settle. Past the lag limit an extra spring (0.86 x the drive's) and extra damping of the lag rate hold the lag near the limit (Ancient Dungeon's own limit damper works on the blade's absolute speed and would let the lag keep growing, to about 40 degrees at 1400 deg/s).

## Grenades

Molotovs, pipe bombs and bile jars are thrown like in Pavlov on a listen server hosted with this build and on the dedicated plugin (feature `HandThrow`, 0x400):

- The grip holds the grenade (draw it from its holster). The trigger pulls the pin (haptic tick). Letting go of the grip throws it from your hand; in toggle-grip mode the next squeeze is the release. Holding the trigger through the swing and letting go of it throws too. Without the pin, letting go drops the grenade as an item (`ManualThrowPinRequired`, on by default).
- The grenade leaves exactly where your hand is, in the command you let go. There is no pin-pull or throw animation. The native grenade waits 0.5 s after the press for its pin animation, and then another 0.2 s after the release (`CBaseCSGrenade::StartGrenadeThrow`) before it appears at your eye; none of that happens here.
- The speed is your hand's own: the last ~80 ms of tracked controller motion before release (`ManualThrowVelocityWindowMs`, blended toward the fastest part by `ManualThrowPeakVelocityBlend`). It is measured at headset frame rate in tracking space, so stick turning can't add to it. That speed is multiplied by 3, Pavlov's grenade speed multiplier (`ManualThrowHandVelocityScale` when you host; `ThrowHandVelocityScale` on the plugin). Your own walking or running speed is added unscaled. The server caps the result at `ManualThrowMaxVelocity` / `ThrowMaxSpeed` (1400 units/s). Overhand throws, underhand tosses and plain drops all work.
- The thrown grenade disappears from your hand at once, and the hand switches to your next item.

On other servers the old path stays: pull the trigger to arm, release to throw with your hand's motion (`ManualThrowEnabled`, `ManualThrowVelocityScale`). Plugins without `HandThrow` replace the native projectile's velocity. On servers that can't take a custom velocity, the native throw is aimed so it lands where your hand throw would have.

## Pills and adrenaline

Bring pills to your mouth, or stab adrenaline into your chest or your other forearm (`ConsumableGesturesEnabled`). The trigger still works. To give pills or adrenaline to a teammate, hold them against the teammate's chest for a moment (`ConsumableGiveEnabled`). On plugin servers the give is aimed from your eye through your hand; elsewhere the mod aims a normal secondary-attack give at the teammate.

## Two-handed weapons

Like Half-Life 2 VR, grabbing the front of a long gun points it from your rear hand to your front hand (`VrHandsTwoHandedAimStrength=1`), with roll from the rear hand. An adaptive stabilizer (`VrHandsTwoHandedStabilizerEnabled`, a One Euro filter) smooths the aim strongly while you hold still and loosens as you swing, so the front hand stays on the foregrip without the gun trailing behind. Tune it with `VrHandsTwoHandedStabilizerMinCutoffHz` (lower = steadier when still) and `VrHandsTwoHandedStabilizerBeta` (higher = less lag while moving).

## Hand poses

The survivor's own hands hold things the way Pavlov VR's hands do (`HandPosesEnabled`, on by default). The finger angles come from Pavlov's hand pose animations, measured per joint and mapped onto the survivor's finger bones; no Pavlov files are used.

- **Gun hand:** keeps L4D2's own grip animation for that gun (it was made around that model) and adds the trigger finger: lifted along the frame when your finger is off the trigger (touch-sensitive triggers, tracked fingers, or the trigger fully released), resting on it when you touch it, and curling a little more as you pull (`HandPoseTriggerFingerScale`, 0 = the animation only). The left pistol of dual pistols does the same with the left trigger.
- **Support hand on the foregrip:** L4D2's own animated support hand for that gun, as before.
- **Held items:** a hand holding a magazine (rifle and SMG, or pistol), a shotgun shell, a pistol slide, a rifle charging handle or bolt, a pump or a bolt-action bolt takes Pavlov's pose for that item, whatever your fingers do, and blends into it (`HandPoseBlendSeconds`, 0.06 s).
- **Free hand:** each finger goes from Pavlov's relaxed open hand to its fist with your finger curls (tracked fingers, or the controller's trigger, grip and thumb sensors); with the grip held and the index finger off the trigger the hand points.
- Only your own first-person hands change. Other players see the finger data they always received, and desktop players see nothing new.
- `HandPoseDebugLog=true` logs each hand's pose changes (`[VR][HandPose] support hand: magazine ...`). `HandPosesEnabled=false` goes back to the older finger curls (`NativeViewmodelLeftHandOpenVR*`).

## Aiming

- **Iron sights:** bullets leave along the drawn gun's own sight line, from the rear sight through the front sight (or along the scope's axis), so a shot lands where the sights point at every distance. The sight points of every stock viewmodel were measured from the model geometry (`vr_iron_sights.h`); each gun is also turned so its sight line is parallel to where your hand points (`SightAutoAlignEnabled`, measured per model after the gun settles). `IronSightAimEnabled=false` returns to the older behaviour (bore parallel to the hand, bullets from the hand). Guns are kept rigid in the hand (`GunRigidInHand`): L4D2's idle sway and firing animation no longer move the gun (the slide and bolt still animate), so the visible kick is the recoil alone and the sights stay on the bullet line while firing. With a calibrated bullet offset (`BulletAimPosOffset`/`BulletAimRotationOffsetDeg`) the calibrated ray is used instead.
- **No crosshair or laser by default:** aim with the sights. The dynamic spread crosshair (`CrosshairEnabled`, or `CrosshairStyle=off`) and the red aim line (`AimLineEnabled`) are off by default and turned off once in existing configs; turn them back on if you want them. The aim ray is still traced for hit feedback either way. Desktop players keep the game's own crosshair.
- **No bullet spread for VR players (`SpreadDisabled`):** on a server running the mod (listen host or the dedicated plugin), a VR player's shot already goes exactly along the aim ray: the server fires along the pose from the VR usercmd, which replaces L4D2's random cone and view punch. With `SpreadDisabled` the crosshair (if enabled) shows no spread gap and the optional artificial VR spread (`VrHandsRealBulletSpreadEnabled`) stays off. Plain servers fire from the eye with L4D2's own spread for every gun; the mod cannot change that.
- **Fixed shotgun pellets (`ShotgunPavlovPattern`):** where the server advertises `ShotgunPattern` (0x800), the client opts in with `l4d2vr_shotgun_pattern 1` and every blast fires the same evenly spread pattern, like Pavlov: one pellet on the aim line and the rest on a sunflower spiral that fills L4D2's own pellet ellipse (PelletScatterYaw x PelletScatterPitch: pump 7.5 x 5, Chrome 5 x 3, auto 8.5 x 4, SPAS 8 x 3.5 degrees) with the same radial density as vanilla, and L4D2's own pellet count. The VR aim already removes the random offset of the whole pattern. The client predicts the same pattern. Other players without the mod see stock pellet tracers. Old clients never opt in; old servers never advertise it.
- **Pavlov recoil (`RecoilEnabled`, `RecoilScale`):** every shot kicks the gun in your hand like the equivalent Pavlov gun: the muzzle rises, the gun slides back toward you, and over a burst it walks sideways; then it settles back. The kick moves the gun relative to your controller, and the aim ray (and so the bullets) follows the kicked gun, so you counter it with your own hands. Holding the foregrip with the other hand keeps the two-handed limits; guns that need two hands (everything but the pistols and Uzi) kick much harder held in one hand (SCAR and hunting rifle ten times as high). `RecoilScale` multiplies the kick (1 = Pavlov, 0 = off). The server fires each command along the pose captured when the command was created, so a shot never includes its own kick. Mouse mode and desktop players have no recoil. Details and per-gun values: see "Recoil model" below.
- **Picture-in-picture scopes:** the scope image shifts with your eye's offset (parallax) and closes in from the edge outside the eyebox (`ScopePictureInPictureLens`, `ScopeParallaxStrength`, `ScopeEyeboxDeg`, `ScopeVignetteExponent`).

### Recoil model

`vr_recoil.h` re-implements Pavlov VR's gun recoil from its decoded mechanics (`vr_recoil_data.h` holds the numbers). The offset is a translation along the gun (cm, negative toward you), a pitch and a yaw (degrees) in the gun's frame.

- Most guns: each shot plays a velocity curve for about 0.125 s; overlapping shots add up (clamped). The integrated offset is clamped to the gun's limits, two-handed `(cm, pitch, yaw)` and one-handed `(2 x cm, 45, 90)`, eased in when you grab or let go of the foregrip. Once the kick stops, a recovery curve brings the gun back over `CounterRecoilDuration` (the slide-back overshoots slightly). A heat value builds while kicking and decays during recovery; it only scales the sideways kick, whose sign follows a pattern clocked by how long the trigger is held, so single shots go nearly straight up and long bursts zig-zag.
- Bolt-action rifles (AWP and Scout): a fixed one-second position curve per shot.
- One-handed, guns that need two hands use Pavlov's unsteady multipliers (angle and translation). Pavlov's rule that drops a bolt rifle fired one-handed is not copied.

## Dedicated server plugin

Build `l4d2vr_server.so` (Linux, 32-bit) with `L4D2VR/server/build_release_linux.sh`, or `l4d2vr_server.dll` on Windows. It replaces `l4d2vr_pose_server`; never load both, or poses are relayed twice.

1. Stop the server. Move any old `l4d2vr_pose_server` files out of `left4dead2/addons`.
2. Copy `l4d2vr_server.so` (or `.dll`) and `l4d2vr_server.vdf` into `left4dead2/addons/`. Optionally copy `l4d2vr_server_config.example.txt` there as `l4d2vr_server_config.txt`.
3. Start the server. After the map loads, the log shows `L4D2VR server: map: ... features=0xFFF` (0x800 is the Pavlov shotgun pattern, which needs the `SharedRandomFloat` hook). A `target X rejected` line names a feature that a game update broke; only that feature turns off.

Before trusting a new game build, run `l4d2vr_server_symcheck <path to server_srv.so>` (built by `run_server_tests.sh`).

## Controls (physical layout)

Both grips are used only for grabbing: they draw from holsters, hold items and take the foregrip. On Index the grips respond to a firm, full-hand squeeze (squeeze value 0.85 right, 0.8 left). The right trigger fires. The left (free-hand) trigger works magazines, shells, slides, bolts and pumps; it fires the left pistol in akimbo and heals with a medkit, but never shoves with a gun. Right A releases the magazine. Push the right stick forward to jump and pull it back to crouch (hold; `StickJumpCrouch`); turning pauses while the stick is pushed mostly forward or back. Clicking the right stick toggles the flashlight, and left X (A on Index) still does too. Oculus Touch, Index (Knuckles) and Vive Cosmos defaults follow this layout.

### OpenXR overlays

Every panel the mod used to show through SteamVR's overlays is an OpenXR composition layer drawn over the 3D view: the main and pause menus, loading screens, the floating HUD (curved, as a cylinder layer when the runtime offers `XR_KHR_composition_layer_cylinder`), the picture-in-picture scope, the rear mirror (also flipped), the wrist and ammo HUDs in both styles, the intent HUD, the kill indicators and the config panel with its menu button. Placement, size, curvature, texture bounds and sort order follow SteamVR's rules. Fading (`SetOverlayAlpha`) uses `XR_KHR_composition_layer_color_scale_bias`; under Proton (which drops that extension's data) and on runtimes without it the pixels are faded instead.

There is no system laser or keyboard in OpenXR, so the backend brings its own: point either controller at a menu, the HUD with a cursor, the config panel or its button and a light-blue beam with a dot appears; the trigger clicks (and drags while held), the stick scrolls lists. While the beam is on a panel that hand's trigger does not reach the game, as with SteamVR. Text fields in the config panel open a VR keyboard below the view: point and pull the trigger on the keys, `Shift` for capitals and symbols, `Done` keeps the text, `Cancel` drops it.

Runtimes may limit the number of layers (`maxLayerCount`, at least 16): when the panels on screen exceed it the kill indicators are left out first, then the wrist panels, scope and mirror, and the log says which (`[VR][OpenXR][Overlay] layer budget ...`). Under Proton a tiny invisible layer is submitted first whenever two or more panels are shown over the 3D view, which works around a Proton bug (wineopenxr corrupts frames whose first layer is the 3D view followed by two or more layers); `L4D2VR_XR_LAYER_WORKAROUND=off|auto|always` overrides that for testing. The log shows `[VR][OpenXR][Overlays]` counters every 5 s (panels, layers, copies, laser events).

### OpenXR controllers

The controls are the same as above. Bindings are part of the mod now: SteamVR's binding screen for the old OpenVR action manifest no longer applies. The mod suggests its own bindings to the runtime for Valve Index, Oculus/Meta Touch (Rift, Quest through Link, Virtual Desktop or WiVRn, Quest Pro and Quest 3 controllers), HTC Vive wands, Vive Cosmos, Vive Focus 3, Windows Mixed Reality, HP Reverb G2, Pico 4 and the generic simple controller (`L4D2VR/vr_backend/openxr_input_profiles.h`). Index, Touch and Cosmos follow the SteamVR binding files; the others get the closest layout: on Vive wands the trackpads are the sticks, pressing the right pad is A (magazine release, menu select) and the right menu button is B (use, back), the left pad press is the flashlight and the left menu button pauses; on WMR the right pad press is A, the right menu button is B, the left menu button pauses and the left pad press shows the scoreboard; the simple controller fires with select and grips with menu. SteamVR's OpenXR binding screen can rebind the actions; Monado cannot.

Click thresholds are applied by the mod, not the runtime: the Index grips need squeeze value 0.85 (right, released below 0.5) and 0.8 (left, below 0.45) as in the SteamVR file; other analog triggers and grips click at 0.25 and release below 0.2 (SteamVR's default), and the Index trackpad press is a 0.25 force. The sticks have a 10% radial deadzone, the menu directions need 70% deflection while the left stick is touched. Haptics use the real duration, frequency and strength. Controller poses are converted from the OpenXR grip pose to the OpenVR controller pose the weapon, holster and hand positions were tuned on, with each runtime's own offset (SteamVR's render model data, Monado's driver data); WMR, HP and Cosmos offsets are estimates. The laser pointer follows the runtime's aim pose (SteamVR's controller tip). The log shows `[VR][OpenXR][Input]` lines: the profile each hand reports, the suggested bindings each profile accepted, and every button change (the first 600).

### OpenXR hands

The hands look and move the same on OpenXR as on OpenVR: the survivor's own hands, the finger poses on held items and magazines, and the finger data sent to other players. The OpenXR backend answers the mod's skeletal reads with SteamVR's own hand skeleton (31 bones, SteamVR's names, hierarchy and open-hand, fist and grip-limit poses).

When the runtime tracks fingers (`XR_EXT_hand_tracking`: SteamVR builds it from the Index controllers' finger sensors, Quest runtimes track the hands, Monado emulates it for some controllers) the fingers follow the tracked joints; "with controller" (the default) keeps the fingers wrapped around the controller (`XR_EXT_hand_joints_motion_range` where the runtime has it), `VrHandsMotionRangeWithoutController=true` lets them close into a fist. Otherwise the fingers follow the controller's sensors as SteamVR's controller hands do: the index finger rests on a touch-sensitive trigger and curls with the pull, the grip closes the other three fingers (the Index grip force closes them the rest of the way, Quest Pro and Quest 3 controllers curl the index finger as it nears the trigger), the thumb goes down on a stick, pad, button or thumb rest; controllers with grip buttons (Vive, WMR, Cosmos) close the hand fully on the button. The fingers move smoothly instead of snapping. The log shows `[VR][OpenXR][Hands]` lines: whether the runtime tracks hands, and for each hand whether it follows tracked joints or the controller's sensors.

These steps (and `cmd=`, `xrinput=`) keep running in the main menu and during map loads.
- Hand poses: `handact=<left|right>:<activity>[:<weaponId>]` forces a hand's pose (left = the support hand, right = the item hand; `free`, `gun`, `animated`, `support`, `magazine`, `shell`, `slide`, `charginghandle`, `pump`, `bolt`, `gunposed`; `<hand>:off` or `off` releases). `posecheck=<left|right>:<activity>` passes when that hand's pose is that activity. `foregrip=1` moves the support hand onto the held gun's foregrip target and squeezes until the two-handed grip holds (`foregrip=0` lets go). `posephotos=1` makes `mage2e` and `shellse2e` photograph the hand on the bolt, slide or pump (`<gun>_bolt_held`, `<gun>_bolt_pulled`, `<gun>_pump_held`).
- All hand-driven reload steps press the free hand's trigger for magazines, shells, bolts and pumps (its grip with `MagazineInteractionTriggerInteractions=false`).
- `sparereturne2e`: AK-47: take a spare with the old magazine seated and let it go. The spare goes back to the pouch, the ammo is unchanged, nothing blocks firing, and the gun fires. Follow it with `mage2e=rifle` to check that switching guns afterwards cannot grant a round.

## Headset test checklist

Test on a listen server you host, on Marmot Cafe with the plugin, and on a server without it.

- Holsters: draw and holster every slot; items visible and glowing on hover; turning keeps them in place.
- Reload every gun type with the off-hand trigger (grips only grab): button release versus the trigger hand pull (AK-47, SG552, military sniper), slaps at different angles, catching, dropped magazines, spare magazine shown only with reserve ammo, chamber only when empty, shotgun shells and pump.
- Sights: look down the sights of every gun (no crosshair): shots land on the front sight at near and far targets.
- Recoil: every gun kicks like its Pavlov counterpart and settles; long bursts climb and zig-zag; rifles and shotguns held one-handed are hard to control, gripping the foregrip tames them; the kick feels right in both hands.
- Shotguns: the pellets land in a tight even square around the aim (servers running the mod).
- Akimbo: each trigger hits where its own pistol points.
- Ambidextrous: draw each item type with the left hand (gun, melee, grenade, medkit, pills, adrenaline): it is in the left hand, aims and fires from the left; hand items over both ways; reload a left-held gun with the right hand from the right hip.
- Switches: no draw animation on any switch (holster, pickup, after a throw, after a medkit).
- Melee: the handle stays in your hand while the blade trails fast swings (katana more than the knife, axe and sledgehammer ring a little afterwards); a wrist flick or a slow swing never swings; a forehand and a backhand right after both swing; hits land where the blade goes and pulse the controller; no swings while walking or turning; thrust and gun bash shove.
- Grenades: hold with the grip, pull the pin with the trigger, let go mid-swing. It should leave the hand at once with no animation, and overhand, underhand and drop should all feel right. Throw distance follows your arm; the arc lands near the target on plain servers.
- Pills to the mouth, adrenaline to the chest and arm.
- Hand poses: the trigger finger lifts off, rests and pulls with your finger on every gun (both pistols with dual pistols); the hand closes around a magazine, a shell, the slide, the charging handle, the pump and a bolt without fingers through them; an empty hand opens, points and makes a fist with your fingers.
- Loading screens and chapter transitions stay live; stats show at chapter end.
- Scopes: parallax and edge darkening as your eye moves.
- A second player sees your body, including crouching.
- OpenXR: run the extra per-runtime items in [openxr.md](openxr.md#headset-test-checklist-openxr) (log lines, controller profile, Index grip and trigger thresholds, laser and keyboard, quitting, no-runtime desktop fallback).
